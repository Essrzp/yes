Yet a Distortion

She woke up among the graves.
But nothing—and no one—made sense.

Part 1

Yet a Distortion

Laduxiana Eris
You stopped around:

Eris was walking along a road in 제천시 (Jecheon), South Korea, distracted, when a hitman aimed for a lethal shot. Just as he fired, someone unexpectedly crossed into his line of fire. He instinctively adjusted his aim at the last moment, causing the bullet to miss its intended location and strike Eris in the upper arm instead.

Eris’s sister had hired the hitman. Eris had inherited ¥95,000,000 in her uncle’s company assets—65% of them. Eris had saved her uncle in a house fire a few years earlier, and he deeply appreciated that.

Eris quickly begins losing blood from the wound and eventually loses consciousness.

An unknown man whose nickname is Dr. Surm happens to be nearby and witnesses the shooting.

Dr. Surm realizes that her condition is rapidly deteriorating. After helping her in the usual way, he realizes she needs urgent medical attention. Emergency services are called while Dr. Surm continues trying to stabilize her.

Dr. Surm reaches into his cape pocket and takes out a small sealed bag. He opens it and takes out an ERY-tab containing a small crystal.

He looks at it for a moment and mumbles,

“ERY-tab 3007… Damn it, I swore I would never use this again…”

He hesitates and looks back at Eris. Realizing the situation, he continues,

“…Lord forgive me.”

Despite his hesitation, Dr. Surm decides that keeping his promise is not worth letting someone die when he may still have a way to save her.

He carefully places the ERY-tab underneath her tongue, making sure it remains there instead of being swallowed.

He watches closely for the response he expects from it while continuing to help her, but nothing obvious happens.

The ambulance arrives, and Eris is rushed to the hospital.

During the emergency transport, Eris briefly regains consciousness. Through her fading vision, she can barely make out Dr. Surm nearby.

“Am I dead?”

“No. What I gave you should keep your consciousness—”

Before Dr. Surm can finish, Eris loses consciousness again.

At the hospital, doctors immediately attempt to stabilize her.

Eris survives, but she does not regain consciousness. She is declared to be in a coma.

Dr. Surm stays long enough to see that she survived. He knows the ERY-tab has been given to her, but he cannot know exactly how her body will react to it.

Eventually, he disappears from the hospital without explanation.

Her friends and family are informed and begin visiting her. Her sister comes too, acting like she truly loved Eris and is devastated by what happened.

Two days later, Dr. Surm returns to the hospital as a visitor. At first, the receptionist refuses to let him see Eris because he has no known relationship with her.

However, an older member of the hospital staff recognizes him from his past as a doctor.

After a short private conversation between them, Dr. Surm is granted permission to visit Eris.

What exactly was said, and why his former connection to the hospital was enough to grant him access, remains unknown.

During a moment when he is able to examine her without her family around, he walks into her room.

He approaches Eris and checks the ERY-tab he had already placed underneath her tongue during the shooting.

The crystal is no longer simply sitting there like before.

Her body has begun absorbing and slowly breaking it down.

Dr. Surm looks at Eris for a moment.

He doesn’t seem relieved.

But he doesn’t seem surprised either.

He turns around and leaves the room.

…

Eris opens her eyes.

Above her is a grey sky.

She remains still for a few seconds.

Cold air brushes against her face.

She sits up.

There is grass beneath her hands. It is slightly wet.

Around her are rows of stones rising from the ground.

Some stand straight. Others lean to one side. A few have fallen completely and are partly covered by grass and dirt.

There are markings carved into them.

Eris looks at one.

She cannot read it.

She doesn’t find this particularly strange.

She stands up and brushes some of the dirt from her clothes.

There is no gunshot wound on her arm.

She doesn’t check for one.

She doesn’t remember having one.

She looks around the grave field.

She understands what the stones are.

Graves.

She knows what graves are.

She knows people are buried beneath them.

She knows how to walk. How to breathe. How to eat and drink. She knows Korean. She understands ordinary objects and basic things about being human.

But she doesn’t wonder how she arrived here.

There is no other place in her mind that she believes she should be.

For Eris, this is simply where she woke up.

She begins walking between the graves.

Some are old enough that their markings have nearly disappeared. Others look much newer.

At the edge of the grave field is a narrow dirt path.

Eris follows it.

She hasn’t gone far when she hears something behind her.

A voice.

She stops.

Someone speaks again.

Eris turns around.

A person is standing several meters away from her near one of the graves.

They say something to her.

Eris stares at them.

She heard every sound clearly.

But none of it meant anything.

The person speaks again, slower this time.

Eris’s expression changes slightly.

She knows what speaking is.

She knows they’re trying to communicate with her.

She knows Korean.

So why can’t she understand them?

The person waits for an answer.

Eris opens her mouth.

“…뭐라고?”

The person stares back at her.

Neither of them understands the other.

For the first time since opening her eyes, Eris feels that something isn’t right.

End of Part 1

Part 2

Who am I?

Laduxiana Eris
You stopped around:

The man near the grave speaks again. Eris watches him carefully. She understands that he is trying to communicate with her, but every sound that leaves his mouth is meaningless.

The man points toward Eris, then toward the ground beneath them. He raises his eyebrows slightly.

Eris stares at him.

The man repeats the gesture. He points at her, then at the ground.

Eris looks down. There is nothing unusual there. Just grass. She looks back at him.

The man sighs and points toward a wooden sign near the edge of the graveyard. Several unfamiliar symbols are carved into it. Among them are two symbols:

٤٥

Beside them are two more characters.

DV

Eris looks at the sign. She cannot read any of it.

The man points at the sign again, then gestures toward the dirt path leading away from the graveyard. Eris walks toward the sign. She looks at its front, then behind it. Nothing. She places a hand against the wooden post and looks back at him.

The man stares at her for a moment before shaking his head.

He tries again. He points at Eris, then toward the ground, then toward the distant lights beyond the graveyard. Finally, he points at Eris once more and tilts his head questioningly.

This time, Eris thinks she understands.

Are you from here?

She looks toward the distant lights and then back at him. She nods.

The man’s expression changes. He had expected almost anything except that. He says something else, but Eris gives him the same blank stare. He asks another question. Nothing.

The man looks at her for several seconds. His expression softens slightly as he glances at the graves surrounding them and then back at Eris. Whatever conclusion he reaches, it seems enough for him.

He doesn’t ask anything else.

Instead, he picks up the tools he had left near one of the graves and returns to his work.

Eris watches him leave. She doesn’t follow. She doesn’t know where else she would go.

Hours pass. The grey sky darkens until the graveyard becomes difficult to see beyond a few rows of stones. Eris eventually sits beneath a large tree near its edge.

The cold bothers her. She pulls her knees closer to herself and, for the first time, properly looks down at her body.

She isn’t wearing anything.

She knows what clothing is. She knows people normally wear it. But she doesn’t remember ever owning any.

The thought bothers her for only a moment. She looks toward the path again. Nobody is there.

Eventually, exhaustion becomes stronger than the cold.

Eris closes her eyes.

…

Voices wake her.

Eris opens her eyes. It’s still dark.

Three figures stand several meters away. Travelers. One of them carries a covered lantern while another has a large bag strapped across his back. They’re talking among themselves.

Eris doesn’t understand them either.

But she notices something almost immediately. Their words don’t sound like the graveyard worker’s words. The language is different.

One of the travelers notices that Eris is awake and says something sharply.

Eris sits up.

The traveler gestures toward her body, then toward the graveyard. His expression makes it obvious that he isn’t happy.

Eris looks at herself, then at him. She doesn’t respond.

The travelers exchange a few words. One steps closer and speaks again. Eris remains where she is.

Nothing.

His frustration grows. He reaches down and grabs Eris by the wrist.

The moment his hand touches her, his entire body freezes.

Something moves through his mana gate. It isn’t pain. It is something deeper and instantly recognizable: a pulse from Eris’s mana interacting with his own.

Normal mana flows evenly. Stable. Parallel.

Hers doesn’t.

It shifts, bends, returns and shifts again.

The traveler immediately releases her.

Eris looks down at her wrist and then back at him. She doesn’t understand why he suddenly stopped.

The other two travelers notice his expression. One asks him something, but he doesn’t answer immediately. He keeps looking at Eris.

Then he reaches forward again, this time carefully, and places two fingers against her arm.

The pulse returns.

His eyes narrow.

He says something quietly.

The mood between the three changes almost immediately. The irritation they had shown before is gone. They begin talking quickly among themselves while occasionally looking toward Eris.

Eris watches them.

She understands none of it.

Eventually, one of the travelers removes part of his outer clothing and throws it toward her. Eris catches it awkwardly.

He gestures for her to cover herself.

She does.

Then he points toward the road, at himself, at the other travelers, and finally at Eris before pointing down the road again.

Eris understands enough.

Come with us.

She stands.

She doesn’t know these people. She doesn’t know where they’re going. But she doesn’t know anywhere else either.

So she follows.

They walk for hours.

The graveyard eventually disappears behind them. Eris watches everything around her as they travel: the roads, the lanterns, the travelers’ clothing and the distant structures she can barely make out in the darkness.

Occasionally, the travelers speak to her.

Eventually, they stop trying.

Eris doesn’t find any of this impossible. There is no remembered world for her to compare it to. No home she thinks she has lost. No family she thinks she should return to.

No Jecheon.

No hospital.

No gunshot.

No Dr. Surm.

There are only fragments of knowledge without memories attached to them.

She knows what a road is. She knows what a tree is. She knows what a person is. She knows that she herself is a person.

But as they continue walking, something begins bothering her.

She notices that each traveler responds when another calls to them. Different sounds seem to correspond to different people.

Names.

Eris understands the concept.

She slows slightly.

She knows people have names.

Then what is hers?

Eris searches her memory.

Nothing.

She tries again, concentrating harder this time.

Nothing.

For the first time since waking in the graveyard, she deliberately attempts to remember something about herself.

Her expression tightens.

Nothing comes.

One of the travelers notices she has stopped. He calls out to her and gestures for her to continue.

Eris follows.

But the question doesn’t leave her.

What is my name?

By morning, they reach a settlement.

Eris notices people staring at her as the travelers lead her through the streets. They keep her between them.

One traveler speaks to a guard. The guard looks at Eris, then back at him. A longer conversation follows before they’re eventually allowed through.

They don’t take Eris toward the markets. They don’t stop somewhere to eat. Instead, they lead her farther into the settlement toward an old stone building.

People nearby seem to avoid standing directly in front of its entrance.

Above the doorway is a symbol Eris doesn’t recognize.

One of the travelers knocks.

Nothing happens.

He knocks again.

The door opens.

A woman stands inside.

The travelers begin speaking immediately. The woman listens without much reaction until her eyes move toward Eris.

For a moment, neither does anything.

Then Eris feels something strange.

Not the pulse from earlier.

Something different.

The woman looks directly into her eyes.

A voice speaks.

Not through Eris’s ears.

Inside her mind.

“You cannot understand them.”

Eris’s eyes widen and she instinctively steps backward. The travelers react, but the woman raises one hand and they stop.

The voice returns.

“Do not be frightened.”

Eris stares at her. For the first time since waking in the graveyard, somebody has spoken words she can understand.

“…You can understand me?”

The woman’s mouth doesn’t move.

“No.”

Eris freezes.

“I cannot understand your language.”

There is a short pause.

“I am making your mind understand mine.”

Eris looks toward the travelers and then back at the woman.

“Where am I?”

The woman doesn’t answer the question.

Her attention shifts toward something Eris cannot see.

Something inside her.

Eris feels pressure in her chest, then somewhere deeper. It isn’t physical.

The woman is examining her mana.

Her expression changes slightly.

She sees the unstable flow.

Then she sees something beneath it.

An engraving.

Something that has belonged to Eris from the moment she first existed.

Birth.

Lineage.

A sigil.

And finally—

A name.

The woman stops.

Eris notices immediately.

“What?”

No response.

Eris takes a step closer.

“What did you see?”

The woman looks directly at her.

“You do not know your own name.”

Eris says nothing.

The travelers glance between the two of them, unable to hear the conversation taking place inside Eris’s mind.

Eris slowly shakes her head.

“No.”

The woman looks into her mana once more and then back into Eris’s eyes.

“I do.”

Eris’s breathing stops for a moment.

The woman begins to speak.

“Your name is…”

End of Part 2

Part 3

Unfortunate Place

Laduxiana Eris
You stopped around:

“Your name is…”

The woman looks directly into Eris’s eyes.

“Laduxiana Eris.”

For a moment, Eris does nothing. Then something changes. Not around her. Inside her. The name doesn’t feel like new information. It feels like something that had always existed somewhere inside her, hidden behind a door she hadn’t known how to open.

Laduxiana Eris.

She repeats it silently, and suddenly other things exist beside it. Twenty-seven. June twenty-fourth. She understands what an age is. She knows that twenty-seven years belong to her. She understands what a birthday is and knows that June twenty-fourth is hers. But there is nothing behind any of it. No memory of turning twenty-seven. No memory of turning twenty-six. No birthdays. No faces. No places. Just information.

Eris looks at the woman.

“…Eris.”

The woman continues watching her.

“My name is Eris.”

“Yes.”

Eris looks down at her hands for a moment. It feels strange to finally have something that belongs to her. A name. She looks back up.

“What else?”

The woman doesn’t answer immediately. Her attention moves back toward Eris’s mana engraving. Eris feels the same pressure return. It moves through somewhere deeper than her body, somewhere she still doesn’t understand.

The woman examines the engraving again. Birth. Lineage. Personal sigil. Name. Nothing appears damaged. Nothing appears altered. Nothing appears false.

Laduxiana Eris is exactly what the engraving says she is.

Then the woman looks beyond it, at the mana moving around it. Her expression changes.

The flow bends. Returns. Bends again. It never travels evenly.

The woman’s eyes narrow. For several seconds, she says nothing.

Eris notices.

“What?”

The woman ignores the question and examines the flow again. One of the travelers says something from behind Eris. The woman turns toward him.

This time, she speaks aloud.

Eris hears the words clearly and understands none of them.

The traveler’s expression changes almost immediately. He looks at Eris, then at the woman, then says something quickly. The second traveler joins in. The third stays silent, staring at Eris as if she has suddenly become something different from the person they brought through the door.

Eris looks between them.

“What are you saying?”

Nobody answers her.

The woman speaks again. One of the travelers takes a step backward.

Eris notices.

“What did you tell them?”

The woman finally looks at her again. Her voice returns inside Eris’s mind.

“Your mana current is irregular.”

Eris waits.

The woman says nothing else.

“…And?”

“In Varethis, there is a name for people whose mana moves as yours does.”

Eris looks down at herself as though she might somehow see it.

“What name?”

“Suvra.”

The word means nothing to her.

“What does that mean?”

The woman hesitates.

“Stained.”

Eris looks back at her.

“I don’t understand.”

“You do not need to yet.”

One of the travelers speaks again. His voice is quieter now. The woman answers him.

Eris turns toward her.

“Stop doing that.”

The woman’s eyes move back toward Eris.

“I accepted payment before examining you.”

Eris stares at her.

“So?”

“My oath requires me to provide the complete result.”

“What oath?”

The woman doesn’t answer.

Outside, someone knocks on the door. The travelers immediately look toward it. The woman says something, and the door opens.

Two guards enter.

Eris recognizes them as guards only because of the way they are dressed and the weapons at their sides. One speaks to the woman. She answers. He looks toward Eris, his eyes remaining on her for several seconds before he says one word.

“Suvra.”

Eris recognizes it now. She doesn’t know exactly what it means, but she understands that it is about her.

One of the guards gestures toward the door.

Eris doesn’t move.

“Where are they taking me?”

The woman looks at her.

“For holding.”

“For how long?”

The woman doesn’t answer.

The guard gestures again. Eris looks at the travelers. None of them meet her eyes.

Then she walks toward the door.

As she passes the first guard, he watches her carefully. The second doesn’t stand as close.

Outside, several people have gathered farther down the street. Eris doesn’t know whether they were already there or whether they came after hearing something. A few notice her.

Someone says the same word.

“Suvra.”

Another person repeats it.

Eris keeps walking. She doesn’t understand why they look at her like that.

She is taken through Dervane until the streets become narrower and the buildings more tightly packed together. Eventually, the guards lead her into another stone structure.

This one has bars.

Eris finally understands.

A prison.

She is searched, given rough clothing, and taken down a corridor lined with cells. Several prisoners look toward her as she passes.

A guard speaks to another man standing near the end of the corridor. Eris catches only one sound she recognizes.

“Suvra.”

The man looks at her immediately. His expression hardens.

He unlocks a cell.

Eris is pushed inside.

Three people are already there. A man sits against the wall with his arms resting over his knees. Another prisoner lies on one of the thin sleeping mats. The third sits alone near the far corner, staring at the floor.

The door closes behind Eris.

Nobody speaks to her at first.

She stands near the bars until the first man eventually says something.

Eris turns toward him.

“I don’t understand you.”

He gives her a blank look.

Eris points toward herself.

“Eris.”

The man watches her.

She points again.

“Eris.”

He seems to understand. He points toward himself and says his own name.

Eris doesn’t catch it.

He repeats it.

She tries to imitate the sound.

Badly.

The prisoner lying down laughs. Eris looks at him. He says something.

She understands none of it.

The days begin to pass.

Eris doesn’t know how many at first. There are no clocks she can read. She begins counting by sleep, by meals, by the periods where light appears through a small opening high in the wall and disappears again.

The prisoners slowly realize that Eris truly cannot understand them. At first, they find it amusing. Then irritating. Eventually, one of them begins pointing at things.

A cup.

He says a word.

Eris repeats it.

He points toward the water inside.

Another word.

Eris repeats that too.

He shakes his head.

She says it again.

He shakes his head harder.

Eris frowns.

The other prisoner laughs.

By the third or fourth day, Eris knows almost nothing.

But almost nothing is more than nothing.

She learns the sounds for yes and no. Water. Food. Stop. Come. You. Me. Name.

She hears the word Dervane repeatedly enough to understand that it refers to the place around them. Later she hears Varethis.

A prisoner points around them, then outward beyond the walls.

“Varethis.”

Eris repeats it.

He nods.

Then he points toward himself and says a short sentence. He repeats it several times, slowly. Eventually, Eris begins separating the words.

I.

Am.

From.

He says the name of somewhere she doesn’t recognize, then points toward Eris.

She thinks for a moment.

“I…”

The prisoner nods quickly.

Eris continues.

“I am…”

Another nod.

She points toward herself.

“Eris.”

The prisoner shakes his head and repeats the sentence again, emphasizing the final word.

Eris finally understands.

Where are you from?

She tries again.

“I am from…”

Nothing follows.

The others wait.

Eris looks toward the floor.

She understands the question now.

That is the problem.

She has no answer.

Dervane is where she arrived after following strangers. Varethis is apparently the country around her. But neither feels like an answer.

There is still no place inside her mind that belongs to her.

Eris finally shakes her head.

“I don’t know.”

The prisoners don’t understand the sentence.

But they understand her expression.

Nobody asks again.

During the week, Eris slowly learns fragments about the people sharing the cell with her. Not through conversation. She still cannot have one. Mostly through gestures, repeated words, and arguments between the prisoners.

One of the men is there because of something involving an oath. Eris hears the same word many times.

Oath.

Sometimes another word accompanies it.

Child.

Then death.

She never manages to understand the entire story, only that a child died and the man’s punishment is connected somehow to oath disobedience.

The second prisoner is easier to understand.

He keeps drawing the same strange symbol into the dirt with his finger.

A sigil.

Then he crosses it out and draws another one over it.

Fake.

False.

Altered.

Eris eventually understands that he tried to falsify one.

The third prisoner tells her nothing. Whenever the others discuss why they are imprisoned, he stays silent. Whenever they point toward him and ask something, he ignores them.

Eris stops trying too.

There is one thing the prisoners all understand about Eris before she understands it herself.

Whenever a guard passes her cell, sometimes they make a gesture toward her.

A hand across the neck.

Sometimes one of the prisoners copies it afterward.

The first time, Eris assumes it is a threat.

The second time, she becomes less certain.

By the fourth time, she understands what the gesture means.

Death.

What she doesn’t understand is when.

She doesn’t know whether she is meant to remain here for weeks. Months. Years. She doesn’t even completely understand what crime she supposedly committed.

Nobody has asked her anything since the Warden examined her.

Nobody has explained anything.

Sometimes she lies awake thinking about the gesture.

A hand crossing the neck.

She knows she doesn’t want it to happen. Her body reacts to the thought before her mind does. Her chest tightens. Her fingers become cold. She imagines pain and immediately stops thinking about it.

But something is missing from the fear.

Eris understands that death means she will stop existing. She understands that it cannot be undone. Yet there is no life behind her that she feels is being stolen.

No person she is desperate to return to.

No home.

No memory of being loved.

No memory of loving anyone.

As far as Eris can remember, she opened her eyes in a graveyard less than a week ago.

Sometimes the thought of dying frightens her.

Sometimes it simply feels strange.

On what Eris believes is the seventh day, footsteps stop outside the cell.

She looks up.

The guard unlocks the door.

Behind him stands the woman from the stone building.

The Warden.

Eris gets to her feet immediately. The other prisoners become quiet.

The woman enters. The guard closes the door behind her but remains outside.

Eris walks closer.

“You.”

The Warden looks at her.

Eris points toward herself.

“Eris.”

The Warden’s expression changes slightly.

“You have begun learning.”

Her voice is inside Eris’s mind again.

Eris almost feels relief simply because she can finally understand someone completely.

“Barely.”

The Warden looks around the cell.

Eris doesn’t waste time.

“Why am I here?”

“You were confirmed.”

“As what?”

“Suvra.”

“I know that word now. I still don’t know what it means.”

The Warden studies her for a moment.

“It is what people here call those born with a current that does not move in the accepted pattern.”

Eris looks at her.

“That’s it?”

“To you.”

“What does that mean?”

“To the Pure Sigil Doctrine, the current is considered a corruption of the human sigil.”

Eris’s expression tightens.

“But you checked mine.”

“Yes.”

“And?”

“You are human.”

Eris waits.

The Warden says nothing more.

“Then tell them.”

“I did.”

Eris stares at her.

Something inside her finally shifts.

Not fear.

Anger.

“Then why am I still here?”

“Because your humanity is not disputed.”

Eris says nothing.

The Warden continues.

“The current itself is the offense.”

“That doesn’t make sense.”

“It does not need to make sense to me.”

“You work for them?”

“No.”

“Then help me.”

“I cannot.”

“Why?”

The Warden looks at her calmly.

“My oath.”

Eris remembers the word from the prisoners.

“Oath.”

“A promise engraved into mana. I accepted mine willingly. I examine. I report what I see. I conceal nothing from the party that paid for the reading.”

“You can’t break it?”

“I can disobey it.”

Eris waits.

“But?”

“I would become an oath offender.”

Eris thinks of the man in the cell. The word suddenly feels heavier.

She looks toward the bars.

“So everybody just does what an engraving tells them?”

“No.”

“That sounds exactly like what you just said.”

The Warden does not respond.

Eris looks back at her.

For the first time, she realizes the woman did not come simply to answer questions.

“Why are you here?”

The Warden remains silent for several seconds.

Eris feels that same discomfort return. She remembers the guards. The throat gesture. The prisoners copying it.

She asks the question she has been unable to ask anyone properly all week.

“How long am I staying here?”

The Warden looks directly into her eyes.

“You are not being kept here.”

Eris frowns.

“What?”

The Warden’s voice remains calm.

“Your holding period ends tomorrow.”

Eris feels a small amount of relief before she can stop herself.

Then she notices the woman’s expression.

The relief disappears.

Eris says nothing.

The Warden continues.

“Tomorrow morning, the Pure Sigil Doctrine will take custody of you.”

Eris remembers the gesture again.

Her hand unconsciously moves toward her neck.

The Warden notices.

Eris looks at her.

“They’re going to kill me.”

“Yes.”

The answer comes too easily.

Eris’s fingers tighten around the fabric covering her legs. She knows what death is. She knows she should be terrified.

And she is afraid.

But the fear still feels incomplete, like her mind is trying to protect a life it cannot remember having.

She looks at the Warden.

“How?”

For the first time since entering the cell, the Warden hesitates.

Then her voice enters Eris’s mind one final time.

“Tomorrow, they will remove your head.”

End of Part 3
