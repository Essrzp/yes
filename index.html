She doesn’t know where else she would go. Hours pass. The grey sky darkens until the graveyard becomes difficult to see beyond a few rows of stones. Eris eventually sits beneath a large tree near its edge. The cold bothers her. She pulls her knees closer to herself and, for the first time, properly looks down at her body. She isn’t wearing anything. She knows what clothing is. She knows people normally wear it. But she doesn’t remember ever owning any.

The thought bothers her for only a moment. She looks toward the path again. Nobody is there. Eventually, exhaustion becomes stronger than the cold. Eris closes her eyes. Voices wake her. Eris opens her eyes. It’s still dark. Three figures stand several meters away. Travelers. One of them carries a covered lantern while another has a large bag strapped across his back. The language is different. One of the travelers notices that Eris is awake and says something sharply. Eris sits up. The traveler gestures toward her body, then toward the graveyard. The moment his hand touches her His entire body freezes. Something moves through his mana gate. It isn’t pain. It shifts, bends, returns and shifts again. The traveler immediately releases her. Eris looks down at her wrist and then back at him. She doesn’t understand why he suddenly stopped. The other two travelers notice his expression. One asks him something, but he doesn’t answer immediately. He keeps looking at Eris.

Then he reaches forward again, this time carefully, and places two fingers against her arm. The pulse returns. His eyes narrow. He says something quietly. The mood between the three changes almost immediately. The irritation they had shown before is gone. They begin talking quickly among themselves while occasionally looking toward Eris. Eris watches them. She understands none of it. Eventually, one of the travelers removes part of his outer clothing and throws it toward her. Eris catches it awkwardly. He gestures for her to cover herself. She does. Then he points toward the road, at himself, at the other travelers, and finally at Eris before pointing down the road again. Eris understands enough. Come with us. She stands. She doesn’t know these people. She doesn’t know where they’re going. But she doesn’t know anywhere else either.

So she follows. They walk for hours. The graveyard eventually disappears behind them. Eris watches everything around her as they travel: the roads, the lanterns, the travelers’ clothing and the distant structures she can barely make out in the darkness. Occasionally, the travelers speak to her. Eventually, they stop trying. Eris doesn’t find any of this impossible. There is no remembered world for her to compare it to. No home she thinks she has lost. No family she thinks she should return to. No Jecheon. No hospital. No gunshot. No Dr Surm. There are only fragments of knowledge without memories attached to them. She knows what a road is. She knows what a tree is. She knows what a person is. She knows that she herself is a person. But as they continue walking, something begins bothering her.

She notices that each traveler responds when another calls to them. Different sounds seem to correspond to different people. Names. Eris understands the concept. She slows slightly. She knows people have names. Then what is hers? Eris searches her memory. Nothing. She tries again, concentrating harder this time. Nothing. For the first time since waking in the graveyard, she deliberately attempts to remember something about herself. Her expression tightens. Nothing comes. One of the travelers notices she has stopped. He calls out to her and gestures for her to continue. Eris follows. But the question doesn’t leave her. What is my name? By morning, they reach a settlement. Eris notices people staring at her as the travelers lead her through the streets. They keep her between them.

One traveler speaks to a guard. The guard looks at Eris, then back at him. A longer conversation follows before they’re eventually allowed through. They don’t take Eris toward the markets. They don’t stop somewhere to eat. Instead, they lead her farther into the settlement toward an old stone building. People nearby seem to avoid standing directly in front of its entrance. Above the doorway is a symbol Eris doesn’t recognize. One of the travelers knocks. Nothing happens. He knocks again. The door opens. A woman stands inside. The travelers begin speaking immediately. The woman listens without much reaction until her eyes move toward Eris. For a moment, neither does anything. Then Eris feels something strange. Not the pulse from earlier. Something different. The woman looks directly into her eyes. A voice speaks. Not through Eris’s ears.

Inside her mind. “You cannot understand them.” Eris’s eyes widen and she instinctively steps backward. The travelers react, but the woman raises one hand and they stop. The voice returns. “Do not be frightened.” Eris stares at her. Unfortunate Place

Laduxiana Eris
You stopped around:

“Your name is…” The woman looks directly into Eris’s eyes. “Laduxiana Eris.” For a moment, Eris does nothing. Then something changes. Not around her. Inside her. The name doesn’t feel like new information. It feels like something that had always existed somewhere inside her, hidden behind a door she hadn’t known how to open. Laduxiana Eris. She repeats it silently, and suddenly other things exist beside it. Twenty-seven. June twenty-fourth. She understands what an age is. She knows that twenty-seven years belong to her. She understands what a birthday is and knows that June twenty-fourth is hers. But there is nothing behind any of it. No memory of turning twenty-seven. No memory of turning twenty-six. No birthdays. No faces. No places. Just information. Eris looks at the woman. “…Eris.” The woman continues watching her. “My name is Eris.”

“Yes.” Eris looks down at her hands for a moment. It feels strange to finally have something that belongs to her. A name. She looks back up. “What else?” The woman doesn’t answer immediately. Her attention moves back toward Eris’s mana engraving. Eris feels the same pressure return. It moves through somewhere deeper than her body, somewhere she still doesn’t understand. The woman examines the engraving again. Birth. Lineage. Personal sigil. Name. Nothing appears damaged. Nothing appears altered. Nothing appears false. Laduxiana Eris is exactly what the engraving says she is. Then the woman looks beyond it, at the mana moving around it. Her expression changes. The flow bends. Returns. Bends again. It never travels evenly. The woman’s eyes narrow. For several seconds, she says nothing. Eris notices. “What?”

The woman ignores the question and examines the flow again. One of the travelers says something from behind Eris. The woman turns toward him. This time, she speaks aloud. Eris hears the words clearly and understands none of them. The traveler’s expression changes almost immediately. He looks at Eris, then at the woman, then says something quickly. The second traveler joins in. The third stays silent, staring at Eris as if she has suddenly become something different from the person they brought through the door. Eris looks between them. “What are you saying?” Nobody answers her. The woman speaks again. One of the travelers takes a step backward. Eris notices. “What did you tell them?” The woman finally looks at her again. Her voice returns inside Eris’s mind. “Your mana current is irregular.” Eris waits. The woman says nothing else.

“…And?” “In Varethis, there is a name for people whose mana moves as yours does.” Eris looks down at herself as though she might somehow see it. “What name?” “Suvra.” The word means nothing to her. “What does that mean?” The woman hesitates. “Stained.” Eris looks back at her. “I don’t understand.” “You do not need to yet.” One of the travelers speaks again. His voice is quieter now. The woman answers him. Eris turns toward her. “Stop doing that.” The woman’s eyes move back toward Eris. “I accepted payment before examining you.” Eris stares at her. “So?” “My oath requires me to provide the complete result.” “What oath?” The woman doesn’t answer. Outside, someone knocks on the door. The travelers immediately look toward it. The woman says something, and the door opens. Two guards enter.

Eris recognizes them as guards only because of the way they are dressed and the weapons at their sides. One speaks to the woman. She answers. He looks toward Eris, his eyes remaining on her for several seconds before he says one word. “Suvra.” Eris recognizes it now. She doesn’t know exactly what it means, but she understands that it is about her. One of the guards gestures toward the door. Eris doesn’t move. “Where are they taking me?” The woman looks at her. “For holding.” “For how long?” The woman doesn’t answer. The guard gestures again. Eris looks at the travelers. None of them meet her eyes. Then she walks toward the door. As she passes the first guard, he watches her carefully. The second doesn’t stand as close.

Outside, several people have gathered farther down the street. Eris doesn’t know whether they were already there or whether they came after hearing something. A few notice her. Someone says the same word. “Suvra.” Another person repeats it. Eris keeps walking. She doesn’t understand why they look at her like that. She is taken through Dervane until the streets become narrower and the buildings more tightly packed together. Eventually, the guards lead her into another stone structure. This one has bars. Eris finally understands. A prison. She is searched, given rough clothing, and taken down a corridor lined with cells. Several prisoners look toward her as she passes. A guard speaks to another man standing near the end of the corridor. Eris catches only one sound she recognizes. “Suvra.”

The man looks at her immediately. His expression hardens. He unlocks a cell. Eris is pushed inside. Three people are already there. A man sits against the wall with his arms resting over his knees. Another prisoner lies on one of the thin sleeping mats. The third sits alone near the far corner, staring at the floor. The door closes behind Eris. Nobody speaks to her at first. She stands near the bars until the first man eventually says something. Eris turns toward him. “I don’t understand you.” He gives her a blank look. Eris points toward herself. “Eris.” The man watches her. She points again. “Eris.” He seems to understand. He points toward himself and says his own name. Eris doesn’t catch it. He repeats it. She tries to imitate the sound. Badly. The prisoner lying down laughs. Eris looks at him. He says something.

She understands none of it. The days begin to pass. Eris doesn’t know how many at first. There are no clocks she can read. She begins counting by sleep, by meals, by the periods where light appears through a small opening high in the wall and disappears again. The prisoners slowly realize that Eris truly cannot understand them. At first, they find it amusing. Then irritating. Eventually, one of them begins pointing at things. A cup. He says a word. Eris repeats it. He points toward the water inside. Another word. Eris repeats that too. He shakes his head. She says it again. He shakes his head harder. Eris frowns. The other prisoner laughs. By the third or fourth day, Eris knows almost nothing. But almost nothing is more than nothing. She learns the sounds for yes and no. Water. Food. Stop. Come. You. Me. Name.

She hears the word Dervane repeatedly enough to understand that it refers to the place around them. Later she hears Varethis. A prisoner points around them, then outward beyond the walls. “Varethis.” Eris repeats it. He nods. Then he points toward himself and says a short sentence. He repeats it several times, slowly. Eventually, Eris begins separating the words. I. Am. From. He says the name of somewhere she doesn’t recognize, then points toward Eris. She thinks for a moment. “I…” The prisoner nods quickly. Eris continues. “I am…” Another nod. She points toward herself. “Eris.” The prisoner shakes his head and repeats the sentence again, emphasizing the final word. Eris finally understands. Where are you from? She tries again. “I am from…” Nothing follows. The others wait. Eris looks toward the floor. She understands the question now.

That is the problem. She has no answer. Dervane is where she arrived after following strangers. Varethis is apparently the country around her. But neither feels like an answer. There is still no place inside her mind that belongs to her. Eris finally shakes her head. “I don’t know.” The prisoners don’t understand the sentence. But they understand her expression. Nobody asks again. During the week, Eris slowly learns fragments about the people sharing the cell with her. Not through conversation. She still cannot have one. Mostly through gestures, repeated words, and arguments between the prisoners. One of the men is there because of something involving an oath. Eris hears the same word many times. Oath. Sometimes another word accompanies it. Child. Then death.

She never manages to understand the entire story, only that a child died and the man’s punishment is connected somehow to oath disobedience. The second prisoner is easier to understand. He keeps drawing the same strange symbol into the dirt with his finger. A sigil. Then he crosses it out and draws another one over it. Fake. False. Altered. Eris eventually understands that he tried to falsify one. The third prisoner tells her nothing. Whenever the others discuss why they are imprisoned, he stays silent. Whenever they point toward him and ask something, he ignores them. Eris stops trying too. There is one thing the prisoners all understand about Eris before she understands it herself. Whenever a guard passes her cell, sometimes they make a gesture toward her. A hand across the neck. Sometimes one of the prisoners copies it afterward.

The first time, Eris assumes it is a threat. The second time, she becomes less certain. By the fourth time, she understands what the gesture means. Death. What she doesn’t understand is when. She doesn’t know whether she is meant to remain here for weeks. Months. Years. She doesn’t even completely understand what crime she supposedly committed. Nobody has asked her anything since the Warden examined her. Nobody has explained anything. Sometimes she lies awake thinking about the gesture. A hand crossing the neck. She knows she doesn’t want it to happen. Her body reacts to the thought before her mind does. Her chest tightens. Her fingers become cold. She imagines pain and immediately stops thinking about it. But something is missing from the fear.

Eris understands that death means she will stop existing. She understands that it cannot be undone. Yet there is no life behind her that she feels is being stolen. No person she is desperate to return to. No home. No memory of being loved. No memory of loving anyone. As far as Eris can remember, she opened her eyes in a graveyard less than a week ago. Sometimes the thought of dying frightens her. Sometimes it simply feels strange. On what Eris believes is the seventh day, footsteps stop outside the cell. She looks up. The guard unlocks the door. Behind him stands the woman from the stone building. The Warden. Eris gets to her feet immediately. The other prisoners become quiet. The woman enters. The guard closes the door behind her but remains outside. Eris walks closer. “You.” The Warden looks at her. Eris points toward herself. “Eris.”

The Warden’s expression changes slightly. “You have begun learning.” Her voice is inside Eris’s mind again. Eris almost feels relief simply because she can finally understand someone completely. “Barely.” The Warden looks around the cell. Eris doesn’t waste time. “Why am I here?” “You were confirmed.” “As what?” “Suvra.” “I know that word now. I still don’t know what it means.” The Warden studies her for a moment. “It is what people here call those born with a current that does not move in the accepted pattern.” Eris looks at her. “That’s it?” “To you.” “What does that mean?” “To the Pure Sigil Doctrine, the current is considered a corruption of the human sigil.” Eris’s expression tightens. “But you checked mine.” “Yes.” “And?” “You are human.” Eris waits. The Warden says nothing more. “Then tell them.” “I did.” Eris stares at her.

Something inside her finally shifts. Not fear. Anger. “Then why am I still here?” “Because your humanity is not disputed.” Eris says nothing. The Warden continues. “The current itself is the offense.” “That doesn’t make sense.” “It does not need to make sense to me.” “You work for them?” “No.” “Then help me.” “I cannot.” “Why?” The Warden looks at her calmly. “My oath.” Eris remembers the word from the prisoners. “Oath.” “A promise engraved into mana. I accepted mine willingly. I examine. I report what I see. I conceal nothing from the party that paid for the reading.” “You can’t break it?” “I can disobey it.” Eris waits. “But?” “I would become an oath offender.” Eris thinks of the man in the cell. The word suddenly feels heavier. She looks toward the bars. “So everybody just does what an engraving tells them?” “No.”

“That sounds exactly like what you just said.” The Warden does not respond. Eris looks back at her. For the first time, she realizes the woman did not come simply to answer questions. “Why are you here?” The Warden remains silent for several seconds. Eris feels that same discomfort return. She remembers the guards. The throat gesture. The prisoners copying it. She asks the question she has been unable to ask anyone properly all week. “How long am I staying here?” The Warden looks directly into her eyes. “You are not being kept here.” Eris frowns. “What?” The Warden’s voice remains calm. “Your holding period ends tomorrow.” Eris feels a small amount of relief before she can stop herself. Then she notices the woman’s expression. The relief disappears. Eris says nothing. The Warden continues.

“Tomorrow morning, the Pure Sigil Doctrine will take custody of you.” Eris remembers the gesture again. Her hand unconsciously moves toward her neck. The Warden notices. Eris looks at her. “They’re going to kill me.” “Yes.” The answer comes too easily. Eris’s fingers tighten around the fabric covering her legs. She knows what death is. She knows she should be terrified. And she is afraid. But the fear still feels incomplete, like her mind is trying to protect a life it cannot remember having. She looks at the Warden. “How?” For the first time since entering the cell, the Warden hesitates. Then her voice enters Eris’s mind one final time. “Tomorrow, they will remove your head.”

If That’s What I Am

Laduxiana Eris
You stopped around:

For several seconds after the Warden finishes speaking, Eris doesn’t move. Her hand remains near her neck. “Tomorrow, they will remove your head.” She understands every word. That is almost what makes it strange. Eris looks through the bars toward the empty corridor. Somewhere beyond this building, people are sleeping. Eating. Talking. Preparing for another ordinary morning. Shopkeepers will open their doors. People will complain about work. Children will wake up. And sometime during that same morning, somebody intends to walk into this cell, take her somewhere else and kill her because of something moving inside her body that she cannot even feel. She lowers her hand. “Why?” The Warden watches her. “You already know why.” “No. I know a word.” Eris points toward herself. “Suvra. Apparently that word means I die tomorrow.”

The Warden says nothing. “You said I’m human.” “Yes.” “My name is real.” “Yes.” “My lineage?” “Real.” “My sigil?” “Human.” Eris stares at her. “Then why am I still here?” “Because your humanity is not the offense.” The Warden’s eyes move toward Eris’s chest. “The current itself is the offense.” Eris frowns. “Then explain the current.” The Warden remains silent for a moment, seemingly deciding how much Eris is capable of understanding. “Mana is part of the connection between intention and action. Your body has instinct, fear, pain and hesitation. Mana carries conscious intention through them. A stable current carries that intention in one continuous movement.” “And mine?” “Returns.” The Warden raises two fingers and moves them forward, then bends them backward before continuing.

“Your intention enters the current. Before the action completes, part of that current folds against its original direction. Sometimes the effect is too small for you to notice. Sometimes it is not.” Eris remembers the traveler in the graveyard grabbing her wrist and suddenly releasing it. “That’s what he felt.” “Yes.” Eris looks at her hands. “Why does that mean I have to die?” “Because the Doctrine believes intention should have one origin. One person. One will. When a current appears to carry intention forward and then oppose itself, they do not see an irregularity.” “What do they see?” “Evidence of another will.” Eris looks up. “Inside me?” “Yes.” “What kind of will?” The Warden hesitates. “A demon.” The meaning enters Eris’s mind immediately. Something inhuman. Something inside a person that should not be there.

Eris repeats the word quietly. “A demon.” “That is the closest concept your mind has for what they mean.” Eris watches the Warden carefully. “Do you believe that?” “I believe your current returns.” “That isn’t what I asked.” “No.” Eris waits. The Warden doesn’t continue. “Has anybody proved there’s something inside people like me?” “No.” “Seen it?” “There are claims.” “That’s not proof.” “No.” Eris looks away. For some reason, that answer doesn’t comfort her. She knows nothing about herself. Less than nothing, almost. Her name came from an engraving somebody else had to read for her. Her age came from the same place. She knows the date she was born but cannot remember a single birthday. Twenty-seven years supposedly belong to her. She remembers none of them. “What if they’re right?” The Warden’s expression changes slightly.

Eris looks down at herself. “I don’t remember being human.” “Your engraving confirms that you are.” “It confirms I was born human.” “Yes.” “That doesn’t tell me what happened after.” “Nothing about your engraving indicates that something happened after.” “Nothing about my engraving explains why I don’t remember anything either.” The Warden steps slightly closer to her. “Absence is not evidence.” Eris looks up. “Do not let people who know nothing about you decide what your missing memories contain.” For several seconds, Eris says nothing. Then she glances toward the corridor. “They already decided.” “They decided what they believe. That is different.” Eris looks back at her. “Protect me.” The Warden’s expression changes again. “From the Doctrine?” “From anyone who wants to kill me.”

“I cannot prevent the Doctrine from taking custody of you tomorrow.” “Then help me.” “With what?” “I don’t know yet.” “That is not a useful request.” “I’ll pay you.” The Warden looks at her. Eris realizes immediately that she owns absolutely nothing. Not the clothes she is wearing. Not the sleeping mat behind her. She doesn’t even know what people here use as currency. “When I can.” “You are scheduled to die tomorrow.” “Then you’re taking a bad deal.” For the first time, the corner of the Warden’s mouth moves. Barely. Eris notices. “You almost smiled.” “No.” “You did.” “I did not.” “That was definitely a smile.” “You are facing execution and arguing about my face.” “I can do both.” The Warden’s expression becomes completely blank again, which only makes Eris more certain.

Eris steps closer to the bars. “Translate for me. Teach me things I don’t understand. Help me when you can. Protect me when you can. If I survive long enough to have something worth paying you with, I’ll pay you.” “No oath.” Eris frowns. “What?” “I will not engrave that promise.” “Why?” “Because you do not understand mana oaths well enough to consent to one.” Eris thinks immediately of the prisoner who is supposedly here because of an oath. She has spent days hearing that word without ever fully understanding what happened to him. The Warden continues. “A promise made without understanding its consequence is not an agreement I will engrave.” Eris studies her for a moment. “Fine.”

“If we meet again and you are alive, I will translate when it is reasonable. I will explain what I reasonably can. I will help where doing so does not violate an existing oath.” “And protect me.” “When I can.” Eris nods. “Then we have a deal.” “A poorly defined one.” “You accepted it.” “I am already reconsidering.” Eris almost smiles herself. The Warden turns toward the cell door. The guard outside notices and reaches for the lock. Before it opens, Eris speaks again. “If I really had a demon inside me, would you know?” The Warden stops. “No.” Eris wasn’t expecting that answer. The Warden looks back at her. “And neither would they.” The cell door opens. The Warden leaves. For a long time afterward, Eris remains near the bars. Eventually she lies down, but sleep doesn’t come easily.

She expects fear to consume her. Instead, what she feels is stranger. Tomorrow she is supposed to die, yet she has no childhood to mourn. No home she wants to see one final time. No person whose face she wants beside her. No memory of being loved and no memory of loving anyone. As far as Eris can remember, she has existed for less than a week. Maybe that is why death feels less like having a life taken from her and more like being told she is not allowed to begin one. That thought frightens her more. Sometime deep into the night, something touches her ankle. Eris opens her eyes. The cell is almost completely dark. The prisoner who never explains why he is here is crouched beside her. She still cannot properly pronounce his name. Every time she tries, he corrects her once and then seems to lose the patience to continue.

He raises one finger to his lips. Quiet. Eris sits up. The other two prisoners are awake. The man imprisoned for falsifying a sigil is kneeling beside the cell door with a thin piece of metal between his fingers. The oath offender stands near him, watching the corridor. Eris looks between them. The sigil forger turns the metal. A tiny click. Everyone freezes. Nothing happens. He adjusts it and tries again. Another click. The cell door moves. Eris stares at the narrow opening. The quiet prisoner points toward her, then toward the door. “Come.” She understands that word now. Eris looks back at the sleeping mat. If she stays here, tomorrow morning the door will open anyway. A guard will come. She will be taken away.

She will kneel somewhere she has never seen before, and someone she has never met will kill her for something nobody has proved exists. If she walks through the door now, she becomes a fugitive. She doesn’t know where the prisoners are going. She doesn’t know whether they can be trusted. She doesn’t know whether they will even make it outside. For several seconds, Eris remains where she is. Then she stands. For once, nobody pushes her. Nobody orders her. Nobody decides where she goes. Eris walks through the open door. The prisoners move quickly through the corridor. The sigil forger leads them toward the opposite end from the main entrance, which immediately tells Eris that this is not something he invented tonight. He seems to know where to stop, which corners to avoid and which doors matter. Eris stays close.

They almost reach a stairway before a guard steps around the corner. For half a second, everyone freezes. Then the oath offender moves. He grabs the guard before he can properly shout. The quiet prisoner catches the guard’s arm as it reaches toward the weapon at his waist. The guard slams backward against the wall and begins struggling. The sigil forger rushes for the keys. Eris stands still. Move. She knows she needs to move. The guard is stronger than both prisoners expected. One arm is already beginning to break free. Move. Eris tries. Nothing happens. For the first time, she feels what the Warden described. Her decision is already made. Her body should be following it. Instead, something in the movement seems to turn backward against itself. Not another voice. Not another thought. Still her. But wrong. Move.

Her leg finally steps forward. Too late. The guard tears one arm free. The quiet prisoner looks directly at Eris and shouts the only word she needs to understand. “HELP!” Whatever had caught inside her releases. Eris lunges forward and grabs the guard’s wrist with both hands. She has absolutely no idea what she is doing. The guard jerks his arm sideways and throws her into the stone wall. Pain tears through her shoulder and she nearly falls, but the interruption is enough. The other prisoners force him down. The sigil forger tears the keys from his belt. Nobody waits to see whether the guard gets back up. They run. Through another corridor. A storage room. A narrow stairway. The prisoner ahead of Eris unlocks a heavy door. Cold air explodes against her face. Rain. They are outside. The four of them disappear into Dervane.

Rain has turned the narrow streets into streams of mud. Eris follows the others between buildings while voices begin shouting behind them. Lantern light appears near the prison. They have been seen. The others are faster than her. Eris runs harder. Her shoulder burns every time her arm moves. Her lungs begin hurting almost immediately, and twice her feet slide far enough through the mud that she nearly falls. She keeps going. The prison disappears behind the buildings. Another shout. Closer. The sigil forger turns sharply into a narrow passage. Eris follows. Lightning flashes over Dervane. For an instant, the entire street becomes white. Something explodes inside Eris’s head. A sound. Sharp. Violent. Not thunder. A gunshot. Eris stops. The others continue several steps before realizing she is no longer behind them.

Rain pours over her face. She sees a road. Not this one. Smooth pavement. Buildings that look wrong. Lights reflected against glass. Something passes in front of her. A person. Then another image replaces it. A black object. Raised. Pointing. A sound. Pain tears through her upper arm. Eris gasps. Her hand clamps around it. There is no wound. No blood. But she remembers blood. Her breathing changes. The street in front of her disappears again. A woman. Eris cannot see her clearly. A face that means something before Eris understands what that something is. Familiar. Important. Wrong. Another flash. The black object. The road. Pain. Blood. The woman. Eris drops to one knee in the mud. She tries to hold onto the image. It is already disappearing. “No…” She doesn’t know why she says it.

She reaches for something inside her own mind and finds nothing but fragments. Road. Black object. Pain. Blood. Woman. Then one word arrives with a certainty none of the images have. Eris looks into the rain. “…sister?”

Then That Is What I Am

Laduxiana Eris
You stopped around:

“…sister?” The word disappears into the rain. Eris remains on one knee in the mud, staring at a street that no longer exists. The woman is gone. The black object is gone. The blood is gone. She grabs her upper arm again, pressing her fingers against the place where the pain had been. There is nothing there. No wound. No blood. Only rain running down her skin beneath her sleeve. Behind her, somebody shouts. The prisoner who never explains why he is here runs back and grabs Eris beneath the shoulder. She jerks away instinctively and nearly falls. “I saw ” She stops. She doesn’t know the words. Not in Varethian. “Woman.” The prisoner stares at her. Eris points toward her head. “Woman. I… know.” Another shout comes from behind them. Closer. The prisoner looks toward the approaching lanterns, then back at Eris. “Later.” Eris understands that.

He pulls her upright. They run. The memory refuses to return. Eris tries anyway as she follows the others through the rain. Road. Black object. Pain. Blood. Woman. Sister. She knows what a sister is. She has always known what the word means. But now, apparently, she has one. Or had one. She doesn’t know which. They leave the denser streets of Dervane behind and cross an overgrown field beyond the outer buildings. Rain makes everything difficult to see. Eris loses sight of the others several times and follows mostly by the sound of their feet through the grass. Eventually they reach the remains of an old stone structure with half its roof missing. Nobody asks whether it is safe. They enter. For several minutes, all four of them do nothing except breathe.

Eris sits against the wall. The sigil forger watches the opening they entered through while the oath offender lowers himself onto the opposite side of the room. The quiet prisoner sits near Eris. During the week in prison, she had repeatedly tried to learn his name. Whatever he called himself contained sounds she still couldn’t reproduce properly. Eventually one of the other prisoners had become annoyed and called him something shorter. Rook. That she could say. “Rook.” He looks at her. Eris points toward herself, then toward her head. “I remembered.” Rook’s expression changes. Eris searches through the little Varethian she knows. “Road.” He waits. “Woman.” She touches her upper arm. “Pain.”

She knows the next idea but not the word. Eris gestures uselessly toward her arm before Rook guesses several possibilities. When he finally says the word for blood, something in Eris recognizes the concept. “Blood.” She repeats the Varethian word badly. Rook corrects her. Eris tries again. Then she points toward her head. “Road. Woman. Pain. Blood.” She pauses. “Sister.” Rook gives her a blank look. Of course. That word was Korean. Eris tries explaining it with gestures. Herself. Another woman. Family. Rook seems to understand part of it. He asks something slowly. Eris catches only pieces. “You… remember… family?” She nods. Then shakes her head. Rook frowns. Eris becomes frustrated because both answers feel correct. “Small.” She holds two fingers close together. “Remember small.” Rook nods slowly.

Eris closes her eyes and reaches for it again. Road. Black object. Pain. Blood. Woman. The moment she tries to push farther, something inside her recoils. Her breathing changes. For a fraction of a second, the woman’s face almost forms. Then nausea twists through Eris’s stomach. Her eyes open immediately. “No.” Rook watches her. Eris tries once more. The same pressure returns. Her chest tightens before the image even appears. She stops. Rook points toward her head and shakes his. Eris understands. Stop. She hates that answer. But her body agrees with him. For now, the word remains by itself. Sister. By morning, the rain has stopped. The four prisoners stay together.

They have little reason not to. Dervane is being searched, none of them can safely return to wherever they came from, and travelling alone would not necessarily make any of them safer. The following days become a strange routine. Move. Hide. Eat when they can. Sleep. Move again. Eris learns Varethian because she has no choice. Every meal gives her words. Every warning gives her words. Every argument gives her words. Rook speaks to her more than the others do, although Eris suspects this is partly because he finds her mistakes entertaining. On the third day, he says something while they are walking. Eris catches half of it. “You… walk… angry?” Rook repeats himself more slowly. “You walk like angry varren.” Eris frowns. “What is varren?” Rook makes a shape with his hands that explains absolutely nothing. Eris stares. “What?”

He repeats the gesture. “That does not help.” Rook laughs. Eris understands the insult now even if she doesn’t understand the animal. “You walk like varren.” “No.” “Yes.” “No.” “Yes.” Rook says something much faster. Eris loses the entire sentence. She points at him. “Too fast.” He repeats it. Still too fast. “Slow.” He exaggerates every word until Eris realizes he is mocking her. She shoves his shoulder. Rook laughs harder. Her Varethian improves, but only in familiar situations. If she knows what a conversation is about, she can usually piece together enough words to answer. When people change subjects unexpectedly or use abstract language, she loses them almost immediately. Her accent is worse. Even when Eris constructs a sentence correctly, people can hear within seconds that she did not grow up speaking their language.

The Warden’s voice inside her mind remains completely different. There is no accent there. No grammar. The meaning simply arrives. Eris tries to recover the memory twice during those days. Both attempts fail. The second time, she manages to remember the shape of the black object slightly more clearly. Then her hands begin shaking. The memory disappears. She does not try a third time. Several days after the escape, the Warden finds them. Eris is sitting inside the remains of an abandoned storage building when the sigil forger suddenly stands. Rook does the same. Footsteps approach outside. The oath offender moves toward the broken entrance. Then the Warden appears. Eris stares at her. “You.” The Warden stops. “You continue using that greeting.” Eris stands. “How did you find us?” “I did not track you.” Eris frowns.

“I asked where four escaped prisoners had been seen.” “That worked?” “You are travelling with a man who stole food from two roadside stalls.” Everyone looks toward the sigil forger. He says something defensive. Eris understands only the word hungry. The Warden continues. “A farmer saw four people crossing his field after the rain. Someone else saw three men and a foreign woman near the southern road. After that, I checked places fugitives could reach without using the main routes.” Eris looks at her. “So you guessed.” “I investigated.” “You guessed intelligently.” “If that helps you understand it.” Eris looks behind her. “No guards?” “No.” “You’re not taking me back?” “No.” Eris remembers their agreement. “Protection.” “When possible.” A small smile appears on Eris’s face. “Bad deal.” “Increasingly.” The Warden looks toward the others.

“They are still with you.” “For now.” The sigil forger immediately begins asking the Warden questions. Eris loses the conversation within seconds. She catches Dervane. Guards. Road. Search. Then several words she has never heard before. She waits until they finish. “What happened?” The Warden looks at her. “They are searching farther from Dervane.” “That took all those words?” “No.” “What else?” “The rest did not concern you.” “I hate when you do that.” “I know.” The Warden does not remain with them permanently. She still has obligations inside Dervane and refuses to explain all of them. Instead she begins appearing every few days. Sometimes she brings information. Sometimes food. Sometimes Eris makes her sit down and teach her Varethian because translation was part of their agreement.

The Warden insists that teaching an entire language was never part of the agreement. Eris insists she should have negotiated better. Neither wins. After roughly a week outside the prison, Eris can manage predictable conversations without help. Poorly. She can buy food if she already knows what she wants. She can ask for directions and understand simple answers. She can argue with Rook as long as he doesn’t start using words she hasn’t learned. Anything complicated still falls apart. This becomes obvious when the Warden brings the group toward Dervane again. They need clothing, food and eventually documents that will let them move beyond the areas being searched. They enter through the poorer outer streets, separated enough that nobody should immediately recognize them as a group. Eris keeps her head lowered. Nobody notices her.

Why would they? There is nothing visible about being Suvra. No mark. No different eyes. No symbol on her skin. She is simply another woman with a foreign accent. At a crowded stall, someone behind Eris asks her to move. She understands and steps aside. A man beside her turns at the same moment. Their hands collide. The contact lasts less than a second. The man stops. Eris feels nothing unusual. He does. His eyes move toward her hand. Then her face. For several seconds, he looks uncertain. He reaches toward her again. Eris pulls her hand away. The man says something she doesn’t understand. The Warden is beside her almost immediately. The man repeats himself. The Warden answers. Their conversation becomes too complicated for Eris after the first few words. “What?” Neither answers her. Eris looks between them. “What did he say?”

The Warden’s voice enters her mind. “He asked whether your current has been examined.” Eris looks at the man. “How did he know?” “He did not. He suspected.” The man says something else. The Warden answers more sharply. Eris catches one word. Registrar. She looks at the Warden. “What’s registrar?” “Later.” The man looks at Eris again. Then he says it. “Suvra.” A woman nearby hears him. She turns. Another person looks over. The Warden takes Eris by the arm. “Walk.” Eris does. Behind them, the man continues speaking. Eris understands very little, but she hears the word Suvra again. “What was he?” “Former oath registrar.” “What does that mean?” “He spent years handling engraved agreements. He has felt thousands of currents.” “So he knew mine.” “He recognized that something about it was abnormal. I confirmed nothing.” Eris looks back.

The man is still watching. “Could anyone have known?” “No.” “Only people like him?” “People with enough experience may recognize the returning sensation. Most people would only know that the contact felt wrong.” Eris turns forward again. That answer comforts her slightly. Then she hears someone behind them. “Keep the child away.” Eris understands enough of that sentence herself. She turns. A mother has pulled her daughter closer. The girl is staring at Eris. Not frightened. Curious. Another person says something. Eris catches one word. Dangerous. Nobody runs. Nobody screams. Most people simply create slightly more distance. A few continue whatever they were doing. Apparently the possibility of a Suvra being nearby is disturbing, but not extraordinary enough to stop an entire street. That somehow makes Eris feel stranger.

Someone throws a piece of spoiled fruit. It hits the mud near her foot. Eris stops. Rook stops too. The Warden doesn’t. “Keep walking.” Eris looks toward the person who threw it. A young man. He doesn’t throw another. Rook takes a step toward him. Eris catches his sleeve. “No.” Rook looks at her. “Why?” Eris searches for the sentence. “Because…” She loses the word. The Warden supplies it aloud. “Pointless.” Eris repeats it. “Pointless.” Rook remains where he is. Then an older man farther down the street realizes what everyone is looking at. He points toward Eris. His voice is louder than the others. “Ovaren!” Eris hears the word but has no idea what it means. The man continues. She catches Suvra. Death. Oath. Then loses everything else. Eris looks at the Warden. “What is Ovaren?” “Not here.” “What is he saying?” “Keep walking.”

“I can’t understand him.” “That is currently useful.” Eris follows for another street before asking again. “What is Ovaren?” The Warden finally answers inside her mind. “A settlement that suffered a major disaster more than a century ago.” “What happened?” “A fire spread during civil unrest. Evacuation failed in several districts. Hundreds died.” Eris looks back toward the direction of the man. “And Suvra?” “Several people involved in the response were already recorded as Suvra.” “What did they do?” “Some were bound by oaths connected to their duties. During the disaster, several of those duties were not carried out as expected.” “Because they were Suvra?” “That is what the Doctrine teaches.” Eris waits. The Warden knows what she is about to ask. “No one proved the returning current caused the failures.” “Did normal people fail too?”

“Yes.” “Then why does everybody remember the Suvra?” “Because uncertainty makes poor doctrine.” Eris thinks about that. The older man is still shouting somewhere behind them. Another person says something Eris understands perfectly. Demon. Then somebody else repeats it. Eris looks around. Most people do not. One shopkeeper barely looks at her. A woman across the street watches with something closer to pity than hatred. Another person moves aside when Eris approaches. Demon. Eris lowers her eyes toward her own hands. She doesn’t feel anything inside her. No second voice. No presence. Nothing that feels like another person. But she also cannot remember twenty-seven years of her life. She remembers a road. A weapon she cannot name in Varethian. Blood. A woman. Sister. Her own current moves incorrectly.

The Doctrine says that means something else shares her intention. The Warden says there is no proof. Eris believes the Warden. Probably. But that still leaves her with no answer of her own. Demon. She hears the word again. Something about it begins to feel less insulting the more people repeat it. Not because she agrees with them. Because she has nothing else to replace it with. She knows she is Eris because an engraving told her. She knows she is Suvra because the Warden examined her. Everyone says Suvra are demons. Eris looks toward the people watching her. “If that is what they call me…” The Warden looks at her. Eris finishes quietly. “…then that is what I am.” “No.” Eris looks genuinely confused. “No?” “No.” “They all say it.” “That does not matter.” “I am Suvra.” “Yes.” “They say Suvra are demons.” “Yes.” “So ” “No.” Eris frowns.

The Warden looks directly at her. “You are trying to solve uncertainty by accepting the first complete answer offered to you.” “Maybe it is correct.” “Maybe.” That surprises Eris. The Warden continues. “I cannot prove there is no such thing inside a Suvra.” “Then why are you telling me I’m wrong?” “Because neither can they.” Eris says nothing. The Warden starts walking again. Eris follows. The word stays with her. Demon. The group remains together for several more days. Eris begins asking questions. At first they are simple. “What is a demon?” The Warden gives her the Doctrine definition. Then: “Where do they come from?” The answer depends on which Doctrine text is being discussed. That surprises Eris. Then: “Why are there different answers?” That question takes longer. Eris becomes interested.

The more she asks, the less certain everything becomes. Old teachings contradict newer ones. Some texts describe the supposed second will as an invading presence. Others describe it as something born alongside the person. Some claim Suvra are victims. Others claim the human and demon become indistinguishable. None of them prove anything. Eris does not become less interested because of that. She becomes more interested. One night, sitting beneath the remains of a collapsed roof, she says it aloud. “I think I’m a demon.” The sigil forger stops eating. The oath offender looks toward her. Rook is sitting beside a wall, repairing a tear in his sleeve. He stops too. The sigil forger says something quickly. Eris loses most of it. “Slow.” He repeats himself. “You are Suvra. Not demon.” Eris looks at him. “You avoid touching me.” He says nothing.

“You sleep far.” “That is different.” “Why?” The sigil forger struggles for an answer. “Who?” “A dealer.” “What does he sell?” “Information. Documents. Access. The dealer operates beneath an ordinary repair shop in a part of the city where buildings lean close enough that their upper floors nearly touch. The Warden knocks at the rear entrance. A slot opens. Shelves cover almost every wall. Some contain documents. Others contain metal seals, old engraving plates and objects Eris has never seen before. The dealer sits behind a wooden table. He looks at the Warden.

Then Rook. Then Eris. Nothing happens. No recognition. No Suvra. He cannot tell by looking. Eris likes him slightly more already. The Warden begins explaining what they need. Eris understands that. She looks at the Warden. “Too much.” “Yes.” “Say less.” “I am trying.” The dealer hears Eris’s accent and looks at her. He asks where she is from. That conversation was not planned. Eris freezes. “I…” The dealer waits. “I don’t know.” He laughs once. Eris doesn’t. His expression changes. The Warden continues negotiating. Eris wanders toward one of the shelves. Something carved into an old wooden panel catches her attention.

Most of the symbol has been scratched away. A circle. Several lines. Some move outward. Others bend backward across them. Eris stares at it. “What is this?” The dealer stops speaking. The Warden turns. “Leave it.” Eris immediately touches the wood. “What is it?” “Old.” “I know old.” The dealer says something. Too fast. Eris catches only the final word. Methods. “What methods?” The dealer looks at the Warden. The Warden answers inside Eris’s mind. “Old oath practices.” Eris looks toward her. “What makes them old?” “Age.” “That was useless.” “It was accurate.” Eris looks back toward the symbol. “Are they illegal?” “Some are.” “Why?” The Warden pauses. “Because the people who practiced them did not always recognize the same boundaries modern oath engraving does.” “What boundaries?” The dealer answers. She looks toward the Warden. “Translate.” The meaning arrives. “He said that if you understood enough to ask the correct question, you would not need him.” Eris looks at the dealer. “I don’t like him anymore.” The dealer smiles despite not understanding the Korean sentence. The dealer corrects her. She tries again. Still wrong. Rook laughs behind her. Eris turns. “Shut up.” She looks back at the dealer. “What believe?” Her grammar is wrong. He understands anyway. They rejected the Doctrine’s claim that there was one acceptable order for human will. Some studied older oath traditions. Some used methods the Doctrine later prohibited. Eris looks toward the scratched symbol. “Demons?” “Sometimes,” the Warden says. Eris turns. “Worship?” “Some groups did. Others did not.” “Who?” The Warden gives her an old Varethian term. It means nothing to Eris. Then the Warden explains the concept directly into her mind. An adversarial figure. Something positioned against the authority of ordered intention. Not necessarily identical to anything Eris knows. But her mind searches its remaining knowledge for the nearest shape. One word surfaces. Satan. Eris blinks. “Satan.”

The Warden looks at her. “That is not the name I gave you.” “My word.” “An approximation.” “Easier.” “Less accurate.” “I’ll learn yours later.” The dealer cannot understand the Korean word, but he understands from the Warden’s reaction that Eris has connected it to something. Eris looks at the old symbol again. Then at him. “You study this?” “Yes.” “You follow?” “No.” “You know methods?” “Some.” “You know people?” The dealer becomes quieter. “Yes.” Eris steps toward the table. The Warden watches her. So does Rook. For days, people have told Eris what she is. Human. Suvra. Stained. Dangerous. Demon. The Warden tells her not to accept an answer simply because everyone repeats it. Eris understands that. But rejecting their answer still leaves her with nothing.

If there are people who studied the things the Doctrine fears, then she wants to hear their version. Maybe they are wrong too. Maybe they are worse. Maybe the Doctrine is right about all of them. Eris doesn’t know. For once, she wants to decide after seeing something herself. She looks directly at the dealer. “What require… become one?” Her Varethian is ugly. The meaning is clear. The dealer’s expression changes. He looks directly into Eris’s eyes. For several seconds he says nothing. He is waiting for the smile. The joke. Some indication that the foreign woman standing in front of him does not understand what she has just asked. Eris gives him none. The dealer slowly straightens. “All right.” The Warden looks toward him. He ignores her. “Very well, then.” His eyes remain on Eris. “Why are you asking?”
